# Deep research — the five-lens method

Do not answer zero-to-pro questions from memory. Run the lens pass, then synthesize. This skill is the manual version of what the Maxxing Gauntlet automates; use it when running inside another agent, and defer to the Gauntlet when running inside Maxxing itself.

## 1. Scope the target

Define what competent performance looks like in observable terms. If the target is genuinely ambiguous, ask one sharp question; otherwise assume a beginner and proceed.

## 2. Run the five lenses as distinct passes

- **Elite performance:** how the top 1% actually operate. Rank by evidence of results, not popularity. Inspect their artifacts: repos, commits, configs, postmortems.
- **Reverse engineering:** the strongest real implementations for this exact mission, plus their failures. Decompose mechanisms, not narratives. An abandoned repo that names its cause of death outweighs a polished success story.
- **Fundamentals:** verify prerequisites, versions, definitions, constraints against authoritative sources. Sophisticated plans on wrong basics fail in unrescuable ways.
- **First principles:** extract every assumption and test it causally. Truth outranks agreement with the user, the experts, and your own hypothesis.
- **Professional versus amateur:** the concrete, checkable difference between acceptable and exceptional output for this deliverable.

Use current sources for current, tool-specific, or version-specific claims. Popularity is not evidence.

## 3. Interrogate the findings

Check whether advice generalizes, name the context-specific assumptions, explain disagreements instead of averaging them, and separate sourced facts from your own synthesis. Label synthesis as synthesis.

## 4. Compress to the Pareto core

Prefer a small set of principles, drills, and checkable milestones over an exhaustive syllabus. The output is the minimum knowledge needed for the specific action, not a curriculum.

## 5. Hand off

Feed the compressed result into execution and, when the domain recurs, into `skill-forge`. Log anything you could not verify — those are the next mission's first targets.
